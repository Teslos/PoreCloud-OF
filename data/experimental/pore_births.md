# Pore birth table — aluminium 0.5 mm, ESRF 2025-11-14

`pore_births.csv`, one row per tracked pore, 6898 rows (6578 born inside the
laser window) over nine recordings: `nomf` x2 and one each of `bx bxy bxz by byz
bz` — **experimental labels**, see the convention section below — all 100 W /
100 ms spot welds at 50 400 fps, 2.2 um/px.

Generated by `export_pore_births.py -d results/tiff`. Regenerate the two sidecars
first with `find_interface.py` and `laser_window.py` (both need the raw frames).

This is the 0.5 mm block because it is the only one with a verified **0.00**
false-positive floor across 13 655 pre-laser frames. The 1 mm blocks carry 2-7
pre-existing pores per frame from previous welds on the same plate, so a large
share of their "births" would be old pores drifting into view.

## Axis convention — read before using `condition`

The letters in `condition` are the **experimental** convention, kept verbatim
because they are data provenance. The simulation uses a different one: the two
are a cyclic permutation, and sim -> exp shifts each letter forward in
`x -> y -> z -> x`. Confirmed from primary sources 2026-09-23 (wiki:
`concepts/field-axis-conventions.md`).

| physical direction | experiment | simulation |
|---|---|---|
| transverse (in-plane, perpendicular to scan) | `bx` | `B||z` |
| scan | `by` | `B||x` |
| vertical (normal to the free surface) | `bz` | `B||y` |
| horizontal plane | `bxy` | **XZ** |
| transverse-vertical plane | `bxz` | **YZ** |
| scan-vertical plane | `byz` | **XY** |

No letter means the same thing on both sides. In particular **the horizontal
plane is exp `bxy` but sim XZ**, not sim XY — sim XY contains the beam axis and
equals exp `byz`. Use the `field_direction` column and this trap disappears.

Two caveats on the plane names. These are **spot welds**, so "scan" and
"transverse" name rig axes rather than anything the melt pool distinguishes. The
real horizontal asymmetry is that one axis is through-thickness of the 0.5 mm
lamella and the other is along the plate — and which coil corresponds to which
has never been recorded.

## Read these before using it

**No z.** Radiography projects through the plate, so `r_um` is in-plane only and
is a LOWER BOUND on the true radius. The bound is tight here: 0.5 mm is the
dimension along the beam, so the unmeasured z is at most +-250 um.

**`birth` is first detection, not nucleation.** Pore recall is ~0.55, and a pore
is invisible while inside the keyhole (bright on bright). Births are therefore
systematically LATE, and the radial histogram is biased OUTWARD — a pore is not
seen until it has left the keyhole. Pores that form and collapse quickly are
missed entirely.

**The time origin is uncertain to ~20 ms.** The last keyhole frame falls in two
tight clusters (4233-4275 and 5265-5285, one camera-trigger configuration each)
while the first keyhole frame scatters over 281-2835, because a shallow keyhole
at ignition is often missed. Only `nomf_..._S0002` gives the nominal 100 ms dwell
(281-5279 = 99.2 ms). Prefer `t_from_kh_end_ms`; treat `birth_t_ms` as the looser
of the two.

**The diameter distribution is truncated, with pile-up.** Minimum is 20.6 um
(~69 px of mask area) and the 25-30 um bin holds 23% of all pores — a spike at
the resolution floor, not a physical mode. **Do not fit below ~35-40 um.**

**`--min-life 5` was used**, not the 20 of the published fate tables; 43.7% of
these rows would survive that cut. Filter on `life_frames` to match.
